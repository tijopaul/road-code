Alcohol & fitness to drive

Blood alcohol level (taux d'alcoolémie), medicines (médicaments) and tiredness (fatigue): the limits and warning signs you need for the exam.

📏 The legal limits

Alcohol is measured in grams per litre of blood (g/L) or milligrams per litre of exhaled air (mg/L). The air figure is always half the blood figure.

Each glass raises your level by about 0.20–0.25 g/L. Your body removes only 0.10–0.15 g/L per hour (≈ 2 h 30 per glass).

🏠 Drinks poured at home are usually bigger than bar doses. Eating, water, coffee or energy drinks do not lower your level: only time removes alcohol.

✅ Good habits: take public transport or a taxi, choose a designated sober driver (SAM), sleep where you are, and take your friends' keys.

💊 Medicines pictograms

Some medicines cause drowsiness, dizziness, slower reactions, sight or hearing problems. Always check the box for a triangle before you drive.

Level 1 🟡

Be careful. Don't drive before reading the leaflet.

Level 2 🟠

Be very careful. Don't drive without advice from a health professional.

Level 3 🔴

Danger: do not drive! Get medical advice before driving again.

⚠️ A drowsy driver is 8 times more likely to have an accident. Coffee, an open window or chewing gum do not help: the only fix is to stop or let someone else drive.
✅ Take a break of at least 15 minutes every 2 hours. A 20-minute nap brings back up to 50% of your abilities. Avoid driving at 2–4 pm and 2–5 am, leave well rested, and eat a light meal.
