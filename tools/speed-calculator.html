2-Second Rule, Reaction & Quadratic Braking Math

Reaction distance rises linearly (~1 s for an alert driver). Braking distance grows roughly with the square of speed—doubling speed quadruples braking distance—and lengthens sharply on wet or worn surfaces.

How to measure the 2-second gap on the road

  • On motorways & expressways: Use the white painted edge chevrons. Stay at least 2 chevrons behind the vehicle ahead (Art. R412-12).
  • On any other road: Pick a fixed landmark (road sign, lamppost, bridge shadow). When the car ahead passes it, count “one, two” slowly—you must not reach it before finishing.
  • Quick ETG mental math: Multiply the tens digit of your speed by 6 (e.g., 130 km/h → 13 × 6 = 78 m), or keep at least half your speed in meters in dry conditions.
  • Heavy vehicles & tunnels: Vehicles over 3.5 t or 7 m long must keep at least 50 m outside built-up areas and in tunnels (Art. R412-12 II, L412-2).

Sign Shape Coding & ETG Exam Mechanics

Code de la Route Error Log reminder: never confuse red-bordered prohibition signs (interdiction) with blue circular mandatory signs (obligation).

Panneau d’interdiction

Circular, white with red border. Prohibits an action or sets a maximum speed ceiling.

Panneau d’obligation

Circular, solid blue. Imposes a mandatory direction, lane, minimum speed, or equipment.

Panneau de danger

Triangular, red border. Warns of a hazard ahead (~50 m in town, ~150 m outside town).

Indication & Panonceau

Square/rectangular blue. Guidance info. A sub-panel (panonceau) narrows distance, scope, or vehicles.
